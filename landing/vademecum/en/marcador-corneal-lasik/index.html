LASIK corneal marker

Instrument with a marking head of concentric rings or radial blades of asymmetric sizes, coated in ink, used to stamp reference marks on the corneal surface.

How it is used

Applied to the cornea before creating the flap in LASIK refractive surgery: the asymmetric marks allow the flap to be repositioned in its exact original orientation at the end of the procedure, preventing rotation or an inverted replacement that would cause irregular astigmatism.

Clinical tip

Unequal-width marks (e.g., at the 11 and 1 o'clock axes) prevent the flap from being replaced upside down: if the marks don't line up, the orientation is wrong.
