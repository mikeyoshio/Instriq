Peripheral intravenous catheter (cannula)

A short plastic cannula mounted over a metal guide needle, which is withdrawn once the peripheral vein is punctured, leaving only the flexible tube inside the vessel.

How it is used

It is the most common vascular access route for delivering fluids, intravenous drugs or contrast, when a central venous access is not needed.

Clinical tip

Its gauge, identified by a color code, is chosen according to the expected infusion rate; it is often connected to an extension tube, a three-way stopcock or a heparin lock to allow intermittent access.
