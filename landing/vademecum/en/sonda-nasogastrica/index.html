Nasogastric tube (Ryle's tube)

A thin, flexible tube with multiple perforations at the tip (typically 14-18 Fr in adults), passed through the nose down to the stomach.

How it is used

Used to decompress the stomach, aspirate gastric contents, or deliver enteral feeding and drugs when the patient cannot eat by mouth; thinner-gauge versions exist for neonates and infants.

Clinical tip

It differs from the already-catalogued large-bore gastric suction tube (32-40 Fr), reserved for rapid instillation or aspiration of large volumes of gastric content.
