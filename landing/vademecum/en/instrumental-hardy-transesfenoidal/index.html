Hardy Transsphenoidal Pituitary Instrument Set

A set of microsurgical instruments for the transsphenoidal approach to the sella turcica, developed by neurosurgeon Jules Hardy starting in the 1960s. Includes a self-retaining nasal speculum with toothed blades (to prevent slipping under the microscope) and a series of angled pituitary curettes and forceps of various sizes for adenoma resection.

How it is used

Resection of pituitary adenomas and other sellar and parasellar lesions via the transsphenoidal route (sublabial, transseptal, or direct endonasal), combining microscopic visualization with fluoroscopic or navigation guidance.

Clinical tip

The commercial term "Hardy-Fahlbusch" is common in manufacturer catalogs, but only the attribution to Jules Hardy (speculum and curettes) could be independently verified; Rudolf Fahlbusch (Erlangen) later expanded and modernized transsphenoidal surgery decades afterward, with no evidence found of a jointly designed instrument set.
