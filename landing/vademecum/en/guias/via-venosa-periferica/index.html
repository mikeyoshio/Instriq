Peripheral IV cannulation

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Techniques
Also known as
Peripheral IV cannulation, Canalización de vía venosa periférica, VVP

The IV line carries fluids, drugs and blood throughout surgery. Choose the gauge for its expected use: larger if rapid transfusion may be needed.

Choosing the vein

  • Distal to proximal, in the non-dominant arm if possible.
  • Avoid joints, inflamed veins and an arm with an AV fistula or lymph node clearance.
  • For surgery, in the arm that will stay accessible to anaesthesia.

Technique

  • Hand hygiene, gloves and kit ready.
  • Tourniquet above the site; antiseptic (alcoholic chlorhexidine), let it dry and do not re-palpate.
  • Stretch the skin, insert bevel up at a low angle and, on flashback, advance the cannula and withdraw the needle.
  • Release the tourniquet, connect, check it flows without swelling and secure with a transparent dressing.
  • Needle straight into the sharps bin; record date, gauge and site.

Monitoring

Check the site every shift and before giving anything: pain, redness, induration or extravasation mean removal. Replace on clinical signs or per protocol, not routinely.

Related equipment

  • Peripheral intravenous catheter (cannula)
  • Transparent film dressing
