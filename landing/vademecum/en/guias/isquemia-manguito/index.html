Pneumatic tourniquet

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Patient care
Also known as
Pneumatic tourniquet, Torniquete, Manguito de isquemia, Esmarch

The tourniquet stops blood flow to the limb so surgery can be done in a bloodless field. Used well it is safe, but excessive pressure or time injures nerves, muscle and skin.

Application

  • Cuff of the right width for the limb, on the most proximal, thickest part.
  • Cotton padding under the cuff, with no wrinkles.
  • Do not let prep solution run under the cuff: it causes chemical burns.
  • Check function and leaks beforehand.

Inflation and time

The surgeon sets the pressure per protocol, often based on limb occlusion pressure. Before inflating, the limb can be exsanguinated with an Esmarch bandage, except with infection, tumour or suspected thrombosis. Record the inflation time and remind the surgeon at regular intervals; the protocol sets the maximum time (often about two hours continuously).

Deflation

Tell anaesthesia before deflating: reperfusion can drop the blood pressure. Record deflation time and total time, and check the skin under the cuff.

Related equipment

  • Pneumatic surgical tourniquet
  • Esmarch bandage
