Surgical wound dressings

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Equipment
Also known as
Surgical dressings, Wound dressing, Apósito, NPWT, Terapia de presión negativa

The dressing protects the incision from contamination and absorbs exudate in the first hours while the wound seals. It is applied in theatre with sterile technique.

Types

  • Adhesive dressing with an absorbent pad: the most common.
  • Transparent film: lets you see the wound; for low-exudate wounds.
  • Hydrocolloid or foam: more absorbent, for heavier exudate.
  • Silver or antimicrobial: per protocol when infection risk is high.
  • Incisional negative pressure therapy: in high-risk patients as the surgeon decides.

In theatre

Clean and dry the skin around the incision, apply the dressing before removing the drapes and without tension so it does not blister. Gloves and materials must still be sterile.

Afterwards

As a rule, do not change it within 48 hours if it is dry and intact; if it becomes soaked or detached, change it aseptically. Record its appearance and any sign of infection.

Related equipment

  • Adhesive wound dressing
  • Transparent film dressing
  • Tie-over bolster dressing
