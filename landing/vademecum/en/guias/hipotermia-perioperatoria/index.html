Preventing perioperative hypothermia

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Patient care
Also known as
Perioperative hypothermia, Calentamiento del paciente, Forced-air warming

Below 36 °C the patient is at higher risk of wound infection, bleeding, cardiac events, shivering and slower recovery. Anaesthesia and a cold theatre both contribute, and it is easier to prevent than to correct.

Before surgery

Take the temperature before entering theatre. If it is low, warm the patient before starting anaesthesia. Prewarming with a forced-air blanket reduces the temperature drop in the first hour.

During surgery

  • Warm theatre while the patient is exposed.
  • Cover everything except the surgical field.
  • Forced-air blanket or warming mattress.
  • IV fluids and blood products through a warmer; warm irrigation fluids.
  • Patient temperature at the start and regularly (per protocol, often every 30 minutes).

At the end

Check the temperature before transfer to recovery; if below 36 °C, keep warming. Keep the patient well covered during transfer.

Related equipment

  • Forced-air warming system
  • IV fluid (and blood) warmer
  • Warming cabinet (blankets and IV fluids)
