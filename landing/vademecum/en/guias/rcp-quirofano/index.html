Cardiac arrest in theatre

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Emergencies
Also known as
Intraoperative cardiac arrest, Parada en quirófano

In theatre, arrests are usually witnessed and monitored, and causes are often reversible. The anaesthetist leads using the ERC advanced life support algorithm (2025 guidelines); this covers the rest of the team's role.

First minute

  • Say it aloud and call for help and the arrest trolley.
  • The surgeon stops operating; in laparoscopy, the pneumoperitoneum is released.
  • Start compressions at once; in prone position they can be done on the back while preparing to turn.
  • Defibrillator pads outside the field; internal paddles if the chest is open.

During resuscitation

  • One person keeps time and records drugs, shocks and rhythms.
  • End-tidal CO2 helps judge compression quality and detect return of circulation.
  • Drugs are prepared and given by the anaesthesia team per the algorithm, said aloud.

Causes to look for

Haemorrhage, hypoxaemia, tension pneumothorax, anaphylaxis, local anaesthetic toxicity, embolism (air, cement or pulmonary), potassium disorders, hypothermia and vagal reflexes. The surgical team can help confirm or fix them.

Related equipment

  • External defibrillator-monitor
  • Capnograph (end-tidal CO2 monitor)
  • Self-inflating resuscitation bag
  • Internal defibrillation paddles
