Radiation protection with the C-arm

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Safety
Also known as
Radiation protection, C-arm safety, ALARA

Fluoroscopy exposes patient and team to ionising radiation. Most of the staff dose is scatter from the patient, reduced by time, distance and shielding.

Time, distance and shielding

  • Short pulses and last-image hold instead of continuous screening.
  • Step away from the patient when screening: dose drops sharply with each step back.
  • Lead apron fastened, thyroid collar and, where available, shields and lead glasses.

C-arm position

X-ray tube under the table and the image intensifier or detector above and close to the patient. Scatter is highest on the tube side, so staff stand on the detector side where possible.

People and records

  • Personal dosimeter worn where the radiation protection service says.
  • Warn before each exposure; anyone not needed leaves or steps back.
  • Ask about pregnancy in patients of childbearing age per protocol.
  • Hang aprons, never fold them: cracks in the lead are invisible.

Related equipment

  • C-arm (intraoperative fluoroscopy)
  • Lead apron
  • Thyroid shield / thyroid collar
