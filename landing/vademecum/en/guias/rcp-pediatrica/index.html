Paediatric basic life support

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Emergencies
Also known as
Paediatric basic life support, RCP pediátrica

In children, arrest is usually caused by lack of oxygen, so breaths matter more than in adults. Review summary based on the ERC paediatric life support guidelines (2025).

Start

  • Unresponsive and not breathing normally: open the airway and give 5 rescue breaths.
  • If there are no signs of life, start compressions.
  • Call for help; if alone, do one minute of CPR before going for help.

Compressions

  • Ratio of 15 compressions to 2 breaths.
  • Depress at least one third of the chest: about 4 cm in infants and 5 cm in children.
  • Rate 100-120 per minute.
  • Infant: two thumbs encircling the chest, or two fingers; child: one or two hands.

Airway

Infants: head in a neutral position; children: moderate head tilt. Each breath should visibly raise the chest, without overdoing it.

Related equipment

  • Self-inflating resuscitation bag
  • External defibrillator-monitor
