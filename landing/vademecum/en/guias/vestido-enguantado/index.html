Sterile gowning and gloving

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Asepsis
Also known as
Gowning and gloving, Closed gloving, Doble guante

After surgical hand antisepsis, the sterile gown and gloves form the barrier between the team and the field. The closed technique keeps the bare hand from touching the outside of the gloves.

Gown

Hold the gown by the inside of the neck, let it unfold without touching anything and slide the arms into the sleeves without pushing the hands through the cuffs (closed technique). The circulating nurse ties the inside at the back. The belt is tied after gloving, turning with a sterile colleague or the gown's card.

Gloves

With the closed technique, the glove is placed over the gown cuff with the hand still inside the sleeve and pulled up. Double gloving is recommended: the inner pair, often coloured, shows perforations in the outer glove.

What counts as sterile

Only the front of the gown from chest to waist (or table height) and the sleeves to a few centimetres above the elbow. The back, armpits and below the waist are not considered sterile. Keep the hands in view, between waist and shoulders.

Changing

Change the outer glove if it is perforated or contaminated, or per local protocol (for example, before handling an implant). If the gown is contaminated, change both gown and gloves.
