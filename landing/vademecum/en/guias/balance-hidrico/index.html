Fluid balance and losses in theatre

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Techniques
Also known as
Fluid balance, Balance hídrico, Pérdidas hemáticas

Knowing what goes in and out helps the anaesthetist replace fluid and blood. In theatre, the circulating nurse is best placed to measure losses.

Inputs

Fluids, blood products and the volume of drug infusions. In TURP, hysteroscopy or arthroscopy, also track irrigation in and out: the difference may be absorbed.

Outputs

  • Urine output via catheter and urimeter.
  • Suctioned blood: canister volume minus the irrigation added.
  • Swabs and packs: weigh them and subtract the dry weight (1 g is roughly 1 ml).
  • Drains and nasogastric tube.

Recording

Record hourly or when anaesthesia asks, and at the end on the theatre record. Report falling urine output or rapidly rising losses.

Related equipment

  • Urimeter (hourly urine meter)
  • Wall suction regulator and canister system
  • Laparotomy pad (lap sponge)
  • Radiopaque surgical gauze swab (4x4 sponge)
