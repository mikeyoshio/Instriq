Electrosurgery safety

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Safety
Also known as
Electrosurgery safety, Bisturí eléctrico, Placa neutra, Return electrode

The electrosurgical unit cuts and coagulates by passing current through tissue. The main risks are burns at the pad or contact points, surgical fires and interference with implanted devices.

Return electrode (monopolar)

  • On well-perfused muscle close to the site: thigh, buttock, arm.
  • Clean, dry, hair-free skin; never over bony prominences, scars, tattoos or metal implants.
  • Fully adhered with no folds; do not cut it or reposition it once applied.
  • Recheck it after any change of position.

In the field

  • Holster the pencil when not in use: it can be activated by accident.
  • Clean char off the tip to keep it effective.
  • Use the lowest effective power.
  • No part of the patient touching metal on the table.
  • Smoke evacuation when a lot of plume is produced.

Surgical fire

It takes fuel (alcohol-based prep, drapes), oxygen and an ignition source (diathermy, laser). Let alcohol-based prep dry completely and make sure it does not pool under the patient before draping. Warn anaesthesia when using diathermy near the airway with high oxygen.

Pacemakers and ICDs

Tell the team before starting: bipolar is preferred; if monopolar is needed, place the pad so current does not cross the device, and follow the cardiology or anaesthesia protocol for reprogramming.

Related equipment

  • Electrosurgical generator (ESU)
  • Electrocautery pencil
  • Disposable adhesive dispersive (return) electrode
  • Surgical smoke evacuation system
