Surgical drains

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Equipment
Also known as
Surgical drains, Redon, Blake, Jackson-Pratt, Penrose, Drenaje torácico

Drains remove blood, serum, bile or air from a wound or cavity so they do not collect. They may be passive (gravity or capillary action) or active (suction), open or closed.

Common types

  • Penrose: soft rubber sheet, passive and open.
  • Redon: multi-perforated tube with a vacuum bottle, active and closed.
  • Blake or Jackson-Pratt: fluted or flat tube with a silicone bulb, active and closed.
  • Chest drain: tube connected to an underwater-seal system, for air or fluid in the pleura.
  • T-tube (Kehr): in the bile duct.

In theatre

Secure the drain to the skin, connect it before removing the drapes and activate suction. Label each drain if there is more than one and record its site.

Care

  • Collector below the insertion site and no kinks in the tube.
  • Check suction is maintained (bulb or bottle compressed).
  • Record volume and appearance; report heavy bleeding or sudden changes.
  • Do not routinely clamp a chest drain; follow protocol.

Related equipment

  • Redon drain
  • Jackson-Pratt drain (JP)

  • T-tube (Kehr tube)
