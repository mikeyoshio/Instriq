Urinary and nasogastric catheters

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Equipment
Also known as
Urinary catheter, Sonda vesical, Nasogastric tube, Sonda nasogástrica

In theatre the most common tubes are the urinary catheter, to drain the bladder and monitor urine output, and the nasogastric tube, to decompress the stomach.

Urinary catheter

  • Clear indication: long or pelvic surgery, urine output monitoring or retention.
  • Sterile technique and a closed system from the start.
  • Inflate the balloon with sterile water only once urine flows.
  • Bag below the bladder and unkinked; secure the catheter to the thigh.
  • Remove it as soon as it is no longer needed: each day raises the infection risk.

Nasogastric tube

  • Measure from the nose to the earlobe and down to the xiphoid.
  • In an intubated patient anaesthesia places it; laryngoscopy helps if it is difficult.
  • Confirm position (gastric aspirate, X-ray per protocol) before use.
  • Secure it to the nose without pressing on the nostril.

Related equipment

  • Foley catheter
  • Intermittent (straight) urinary catheter
  • Urine drainage bag
  • Nasogastric tube (Ryle's tube)
