Surgical smoke

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Safety
Also known as
Surgical smoke, Surgical plume, Humo de electrocirugía

Electrosurgery, lasers and ultrasonic devices vaporise tissue and produce smoke containing fine particles, irritant gases and sometimes biological material. The whole team is exposed every day.

How it is controlled

  • Smoke evacuator with the nozzle a few centimetres from the source.
  • Electrosurgical pencil with built-in suction where available.
  • In laparoscopy, filter the outflow gas instead of venting it into the room.

Personal protection

A surgical mask does not filter fine particles well. For smoky procedures or where the plume may carry virus (laser on warts, for example), protocol may call for an FFP2 or FFP3 respirator.

Maintenance

Evacuator filters have a limited life: change them per the indicator or manufacturer, wearing gloves, and discard as biological waste.

Related equipment

  • Surgical smoke evacuation system
  • FFP2/FFP3 (N95-equivalent) respirator for aerosol-generating procedures
  • Electrosurgical generator (ESU)
