Bandages, splints and casts

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Techniques
Also known as
Bandaging, Splints and casts, Vendajes y yesos

They immobilise and protect, but poorly applied they compress. In trauma theatres they are often applied just before the patient wakes.

Principles

  • Distal to proximal, even pressure, fingers or toes left visible.
  • Pad bony prominences and separate skin surfaces that touch.
  • Limb in a functional position unless the surgeon says otherwise.

Plaster and splints

Wet the rolls in lukewarm or cold water: hot water speeds setting and gives off more heat. Mould with the palm, never fingertips, which leave pressure points. After surgery or recent injury a splint rather than a closed cast is often used to allow for swelling.

Neurovascular checks

Check colour, warmth, pulse, sensation and movement of the digits, and pain. Disproportionate pain that worsens on passive stretch may be compartment syndrome: an emergency — report it and loosen the dressing.

Related equipment

  • Oscillating cast saw
  • Cast spreader (Wolff-type)
  • Gel positioning pads
