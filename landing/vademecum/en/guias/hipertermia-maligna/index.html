Malignant hyperthermia

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Safety
Also known as
Malignant hyperthermia, MH, Hipertermia maligna

A rare inherited reaction to volatile anaesthetics and suxamethonium that sends muscle into a hypermetabolic state. It is an emergency treated with dantrolene, the specific drug, which must be available in theatre.

Signs

Usually the first sign is rising end-tidal CO2 that does not fall with more ventilation. Then tachycardia, muscle rigidity (especially masseter), sweating; temperature rises late.

What the nurse does

  • Raise the alarm and bring the MH trolley; call for help: preparing dantrolene takes several people.
  • The anaesthetist stops volatile agents and ventilates with high-flow oxygen.
  • Cool the patient: cold fluids, ice to axillae and groins, warming off.
  • Urinary catheter for urine output and draw the blood tests requested.

Trolley

It holds dantrolene, water for injection to dissolve it, large syringes and needles, cold fluids and blood-test kit. Quantities and dosing follow the hospital protocol; check it like any emergency trolley.

Related equipment

  • Capnograph (end-tidal CO2 monitor)
  • IV fluid (and blood) warmer
