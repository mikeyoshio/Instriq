Handling surgical specimens

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Area
Safety
Also known as
Surgical specimens, Anatomía patológica, Biopsia intraoperatoria, Frozen section

A lost, mislabelled or badly preserved specimen can mean repeat surgery or a wrong diagnosis. Each specimen is identified, preserved and recorded at the time.

Identification

  • The surgeon states aloud what the specimen is, the side and site.
  • Label on the container (not the lid) with two patient identifiers, specimen type, side and date.
  • Orientation markers (stitches, clips) as the surgeon indicates, noted on the request.
  • Read the label aloud at sign out.

Preservation

  • Routine histology: formalin, in a much larger volume than the specimen so it is fully covered.
  • Frozen section: fresh, with no formalin or saline, and sent immediately.
  • Microbiology: sterile container, never formalin.
  • One container per specimen; do not mix lymph nodes from different sites.

Recording

Record each specimen in the theatre log and check the number of containers matches the number of requests. Do not discard anything from the field until it is confirmed not to be a specimen.

Related equipment

  • Specimen retrieval bag
