Handheld dermatoscope

A portable optical instrument combining a magnifying lens (typically around 10x) with its own polarized and/or non-polarized light source, allowing visualization of epidermal and dermoepidermal junction structures by eliminating surface reflection.

How it is used

Used routinely in clinic, either directly or attached to a smartphone camera, to examine pigmented and non-pigmented lesions and distinguish benign features from those suspicious for malignancy (e.g. melanoma), guiding the decision to biopsy; distinct from the already-catalogued AI-assisted total-body digital imaging system, which is oriented toward mapping and photographic follow-up of multiple lesions over time.

Clinical tip

Polarized light allows dry examination of the lesion without the need for contact oil or gel, although some structures (such as milia-like cysts) are better visualized with non-polarized light and direct contact.
