Supine position

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Family
Supine
Also known as
Supine, Dorsal decubitus

Patient lying on the back, head aligned with the trunk on a low pillow, legs straight and slightly apart, arms at the sides or on arm boards at less than 90°.

Where it is used

The most common position: open and laparoscopic abdominal surgery, breast, limbs, neck, face and much vascular surgery.

Risks and pressure points

Pressure on the occiput, scapulae, elbows, sacrum and heels. Brachial plexus injury if the arm is abducted beyond 90° or rotated backwards, and ulnar nerve injury if the elbow rests on the medial epicondyle.

Care and checks

Pad under the knees so they are not hyperextended, heels off-loaded. Palm up on the arm board. No skin touching metal parts of the table. Electrosurgical return pad on a clean, dry, muscular area.

Related equipment

  • Arm board
  • Head ring
  • Gel positioning pads
  • Patient safety strap
  • Anaesthesia screen
  • Operating table (electrohydraulic base unit)
