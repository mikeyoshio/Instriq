Trendelenburg position

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Family
Supine
Also known as
Trendelenburg, Head-down

Supine variant with the table tilted head-down so the pelvis is higher than the head. In steep Trendelenburg (robotic surgery) the tilt may reach 30-45°.

Where it is used

Pelvic and lower abdominal surgery: gynaecology, urology (robotic prostatectomy), laparoscopic colorectal surgery. Also to help cannulate the jugular or subclavian vein.

Risks and pressure points

The patient may slide towards the head. Raised intracranial and intraocular pressure, facial and airway oedema, and reduced lung expansion as the viscera push on the diaphragm. Brachial plexus injury from badly placed shoulder supports.

Care and checks

Secure the patient before tilting with an anti-slip mattress (gel or vacuum), never with shoulder braces against the acromion. Tilt gradually and tell anaesthesia. Recheck the patient after tilting and protect the eyes from oedema.

Related equipment

  • Shoulder supports
  • Vacuum positioning mattress
  • Patient safety strap
  • Operating table (electrohydraulic base unit)
