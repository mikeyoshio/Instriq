Neurosurgical sitting position

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Family
Sitting
Also known as
Sitting position, Neurosurgical sitting position

Patient sitting with the trunk almost upright, head flexed forward and fixed to the table in a pin head holder (Mayfield type), legs raised to heart level and arms resting on the lap or a pillow.

Where it is used

Posterior fossa and posterior cervical spine surgery, when the surgeon needs a dry field and good venous drainage.

Risks and pressure points

Venous air embolism (the main risk), hypotension, pneumocephalus, airway or spinal cord compression from excessive neck flexion, and sciatic nerve injury if the legs are kept straight.

Care and checks

Leave at least two finger-breadths between chin and sternum. Knees flexed. Monitoring for air embolism per the anaesthesia protocol. Saline ready to flood the field if air is entrained. Check the head holder before moving the table.

Related equipment

  • Mayfield skull clamp (head holder)
  • Table footboard
  • Gel positioning pads
