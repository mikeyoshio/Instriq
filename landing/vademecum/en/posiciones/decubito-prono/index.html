Prone position

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Family
Prone
Also known as
Prone, Ventral decubitus

Patient face down, head turned to the side or face-down in a cut-out support (prone pillow or Mayfield head holder), chest and pelvis on supports or a frame that leaves the abdomen free, and the arms at the sides or forward at less than 90°.

Where it is used

Spinal surgery, posterior fossa, back of the limbs, sacral and gluteal region, and some percutaneous kidney surgery.

Risks and pressure points

Pressure on the eyes (ischaemic vision loss), forehead, chin, breasts, genitals, iliac crests, knees and toes. A compressed abdomen reduces venous return and increases bleeding. Tube disconnection or displacement when turning.

Care and checks

Log-roll with enough staff and anaesthesia at the head. Check the eyes and face are free of pressure after turning and periodically. Abdomen free, breasts and genitals uncompressed, toes hanging free or on a pillow. Keep the trolley ready to return the patient to supine in an emergency.

Related equipment

  • Prone face pillow
  • Positioning roll
  • Wilson frame
  • Horseshoe headrest
  • Gel positioning pads
  • Arm board
