Jackknife (Kraske) position

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Family
Prone
Also known as
Jackknife, Kraske position

Prone variant with the table flexed at the hips so the pelvis is the highest point and the head and legs are lower. The buttocks are separated with adhesive tape.

Where it is used

Proctological surgery: haemorrhoids, anal fistulas and fissures, pilonidal sinus and some transanal rectal surgery.

Risks and pressure points

As for prone, with more pressure on the iliac crests and knees. Reduced venous return and hypotension when the table is flexed.

Care and checks

Pad under the pelvis at the table break. Remove the buttock tape carefully at the end without stripping skin. Same face, eye and chest checks as for prone.

Related equipment

  • Prone face pillow
  • Positioning roll
  • Gel positioning pads
  • Patient safety strap
