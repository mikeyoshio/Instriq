Lateral decubitus position

Educational reference: always follow your hospital's protocol and the anaesthesia and surgical team's instructions.

Family
Lateral
Also known as
Lateral decubitus, Lateral position

Patient on one side (named after the side underneath: right lateral if lying on the right side), lower leg flexed, upper leg straight, with a pillow between them. The trunk is held with supports or a vacuum mattress.

Where it is used

Thoracic surgery (thoracotomy, VATS), hip, shoulder, kidney and some spinal approaches.

Risks and pressure points

Pressure on the ear, shoulder, iliac crest, knee and ankle on the lower side. Brachial plexus injury and axillary compression on the lower side. Ventilation-perfusion mismatch: the lower lung gets more blood but ventilates less.

Care and checks

Pad or roll under the chest, just below the armpit (not in it), to off-load the brachial plexus. Head aligned with the spine and the ear not folded. Upper arm on a support without stretching. Log-roll in coordination with anaesthesia, who controls the head and tube.

Related equipment

  • Vacuum positioning mattress
  • Lateral hip and chest supports
  • Positioning roll
  • Double arm board for lateral position
  • Gel positioning pads
  • Patient safety strap
