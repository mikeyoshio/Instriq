Atraumatic (swaged) surgical needle

A needle with no eye, in which the suture thread is factory-pressed (swaged) into a channel at the rear end, forming a continuous needle-thread unit; it may be channeled (flattened, grooved head) or drilled (internal channel).

How it is used

Eliminates the doubled strand of thread that a traditional eyed needle drags through tissue (less trauma and bleeding along the suture track); it is the near-universal construction of modern sutures.

Clinical tip

Laser-welding the thread to the needle is specifically designed to reduce the risk of needle-thread fracture or separation at the point of tension.
