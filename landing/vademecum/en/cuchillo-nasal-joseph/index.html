Joseph nasal knife

A long, slender-handled instrument with a small single-edged cutting blade at the tip, straight or slightly curved, available in a blunt button-tip (protected) variant and a sharp-tip variant.

How it is used

Used in open or closed rhinoplasty: the button-tip variant separates the skin-soft tissue envelope from the underlying cartilage and bone without perforating it, while the sharp-tip variant cuts or shaves the septal and dorsal cartilage with precision.

Clinical tip

Named after Jacques Joseph (1865-1934), considered the father of modern aesthetic rhinoplasty; distinct from the already-catalogued Joseph scissors and Joseph hook (single-blade cutting action, not a shearing or traction instrument).
