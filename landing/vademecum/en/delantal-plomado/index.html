Lead apron

A flexible radiation-protection garment made of lead sheeting or lead-composite material (barium, tungsten, or bismuth compounds) encapsulated between layers of vinyl or durable fabric, closed at the neck and waist.

How it is used

Worn during fluoroscopy- or C-arm-guided procedures (vascular, urologic, and orthopedic/trauma surgery using intraoperative imaging) to attenuate scattered radiation reaching the torso, gonads, and active bone marrow of staff working near the X-ray source.

Clinical tip

The apron only protects the surface it covers, and its attenuation degrades if folded or stored incorrectly (always hang it on a proper rack); it should be inspected periodically for cracks in the internal shielding and paired with a thyroid collar whenever the neck remains exposed.
