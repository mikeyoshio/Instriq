Sluder-Ballenger tonsillotome

A guillotine-type instrument for tonsillectomy: a handle with an oval window (fenestra) that captures the tonsil, and a blade that slides and closes across the opening, excising it in a single motion; mechanically similar to the already-catalogued La Force adenotome, but sized and shaped for the palatine tonsil rather than adenoid tissue.

How it is used

Guillotine tonsillectomy: the tonsil is pushed forward onto the mandible's alveolar eminence so it seats in the instrument's fenestra, which is then closed to excise it within seconds; distinct from the wire snare (Eve-type, already catalogued), which is tightened around the dissected lower pole instead.

Clinical tip

A historic technique (Sluder, 1911) that is very fast but offers limited visual control; today it has largely been superseded by cold-dissection or electrosurgical dissection tonsillectomy, though it is still taught and occasionally used in some centers.
