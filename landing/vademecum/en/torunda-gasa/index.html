Gauze pledget (torunda ball)

A small ball or folded wad of sterile gauze, typically mounted in the jaws of a clamp (such as a sponge-holding forceps or mosquito clamp) rather than held directly by hand.

How it is used

Used to dab or clean small areas of the surgical field, apply antiseptic inside a cavity or wound, or absorb small amounts of blood in confined spaces where a full-size sponge is impractical.

Clinical tip

Never leave it loose in a wound or cavity unmounted in a clamp; due to its small size it is a recognized retained-foreign-body risk and is therefore always included in the formal sponge count.
