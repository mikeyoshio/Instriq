Desjardins gallstone forceps

A long, slender forceps with curved, fenestrated jaws and a ratcheted handle lock; available in several lengths (22-24 cm) and degrees of curvature to follow the course of the common bile duct.

How it is used

Introduced through a choledochotomy to directly grasp and remove stones from the common bile duct during open bile duct exploration; complements the already-catalogued Fogarty biliary balloon catheter (which sweeps the stone out rather than grasping it) and the choledochoscope (which only visualizes), rather than duplicating them.

Clinical tip

The Desjardins pattern is among the most classic and is still taught for open bile duct exploration, though today most residual stones are managed with postoperative ERCP or choledochoscopy.
